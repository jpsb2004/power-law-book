The Power
Law Bookcompute, and the energy it eats

Twenty positions built on one claim: that the demand curve for computation is growing faster than the physical systems that feed it — fuel, molecules, land and grid — can be rebuilt. Each bucket is a different link in that chain, and each position carries the condition that would prove it wrong.

The argument

The buckets are ordered because they depend on each other. Fuel, molecules and land are upstream of compute; and the third bucket exists because the first two could turn out to be one trade wearing two hats.

Performance

The book indexed to 100 twelve months ago, in USD, rebalanced daily to target weight. This is a backward-looking simulation of the current weights, not a track record — the positions were chosen with the benefit of knowing this window.

Allocation

Hedging it away is not free

A hedged return is not the local return. Rolling a short forward on the currency earns or pays the gap between the two interest rates, so a dollar investor hedging reais gives up roughly the Selic-minus-Fed spread every year. The toggle above the charts uses these rates.

Method & limitations

The parts of this that a careful reader should push on. They are here because leaving them out would make the numbers above look more certain than they are.

  • One line is not directly buyable

    The KOSPI is an index, so a real book expresses it through a tracking fund or futures and eats the tracking error and FX. Gold is held as spot-backed shares rather than a dated future, so there is no roll and no expiry to manage.

  • The hedged view is a model, not a quote

    It accrues the policy-rate differential day by day on the full marked value. Real forward points also carry cross-currency basis, and KRW, TWD and BRL hedge offshore through non-deliverable forwards whose implied rates can sit well away from the onshore policy rate.

  • Not every position covers the full window

    A fund that listed part-way through the year has no history before it existed. The curve renormalises across whatever is trading on each date, so the headline return describes the positions that were actually present — not always the whole book.

  • Correlations are biased toward zero

    Taipei and Seoul close before New York opens, so daily returns for those lines are measured against a different information set. Cross-market correlations here read lower than the true economic relationship; weekly sampling would reduce it.

  • Daily rebalancing is a modelling choice

    The curve rebalances to target weight every session. A held book would drift with its winners, so this understates the concentration that would actually have built up.

  • Total return, before your costs

    Returns include reinvested dividends and are net of each fund's own fee, which is already in its price. They are gross of commission, spread, withholding on Brazilian and Saudi dividends, and the FX spread on five currencies. The lived return would be lower.

  • One window, chosen after the fact

    Twelve months, ending on the day the note was written, for positions selected today. That is the most flattering possible framing, and the reason the drawdown column matters more than the return column.
